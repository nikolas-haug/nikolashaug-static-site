# The niche phenomenon of hyperdiegesis in worldbuilding

*What is the opposite of lukewarm or watered-down immersion in fiction or table top gaming settings?*

There's a term from media studies — *hyperdiegesis* — that I think about a lot when I'm building worlds. It was coined by Matt Hills in his book *Fan Cultures* (2002) to describe the creation of a vast and detailed narrative space, only a fraction of which is ever directly seen or described by the audience.

Tolkien is the obvious example. You read *The Lord of the Rings* and you feel, with absolute conviction, that there is a world beyond the edges of the page. The history exists. The languages exist. The wars happened. You didn't see most of it, but it *was there*.

That feeling — that sense of depth, of a world extending in all directions beyond what you can directly observe — is hyperdiegesis.

The opposite of hyperdiegesis is a world that feels assembled only for the purposes of the plot. Characters appear exactly when needed. Geography is conveniently arranged. History begins where the story begins. This kind of world is functional, but it rarely generates the emotional resonance of a world that feels genuinely large.
